Festivals

Belize food festivals and edible experiences

In Belize, food is a reason to gather. Time your trip around these and eat your way deeper into the culture.

Lobsterfest (July)

The summer highlight for seafood lovers. As lobster season opens on July 1, San Pedro, Caye Caulker and Placencia each hold celebrations with beach parties, live music and vendors serving lobster every way imaginable: grilled, curried, in tacos or in buttery Creole sauce. Dates move each year, so check local news before you book.

Garifuna Settlement Day (November 19)

The southern coast around Dangriga and Hopkins comes alive with drumming, dancing, a re-enactment of the Garifuna arrival by boat, and traditional cooking. It is the best time to taste hudut, cassava bread and coconut stews where the culture is strongest. Book rooms early.

Chocolate Festival of Belize (Toledo)

Held in the Toledo District, home to much of Belize’s cacao, it celebrates the Maya legacy of chocolate with tastings, demonstrations and farm visits. A good excuse to explore the deep south.

Crooked Tree Cashew Festival

The village of Crooked Tree, inland from Belize City, honors its cashew harvest with cashew wine, roasted nuts, stewed cashew fruit and music.

Cooking classes and food tours

Some of the best meals you will have are ones you help make. Garifuna cooking experiences in Hopkins teach you to grate coconut and build hudut by hand. Cacao farms near San Ignacio and Punta Gorda offer bean-to-bar sessions. Guided food walks in San Pedro and Placencia lead from street stalls to kitchens you would never find alone.

Planning around a festival

  • Book rooms early: festival weekends fill quickly, especially in Placencia, Caye Caulker and Dangriga.
  • Confirm dates in local news; many events are set only a few weeks ahead.
  • Bring small Belize dollar notes for food stalls.
  • Expect road traffic and fuller water taxis around Lobsterfest and Settlement Day.
