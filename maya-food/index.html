Maya food: the oldest roots in Belize

Corn, beans, squash, chiles and cacao have fed this land for thousands of years, and Maya communities still cook with them.

Belize sits in the heart of the ancient Maya world, with great cities such as Caracol, Xunantunich and Lamanai. Today’s Maya communities (Yucatec, Mopan and Q’eqchi’) live mostly in the Toledo, Cayo and northern districts. Their food is simple and seasonal, built on the milpa, the field where corn, beans and squash grow together.

The milpa

A milpa is a field where corn, beans and squash grow together. The beans climb the cornstalks and enrich the soil; the squash leaves shade out weeds. Chiles, herbs and root crops often grow alongside. This system has fed Maya families for centuries, and much of what you eat in a Maya home comes straight from it.

Corn at every meal

Hand-pressed corn tortillas are the everyday bread, naturally gluten-free. Corn also becomes tamales and bollos, masa filled with seasoned chicken or pork, wrapped in banana or waha leaves and steamed, and atole, a warm corn drink.

Caldo and soups

Caldo is a light, restorative soup, usually chicken with vegetables and spices, eaten with tortillas. In Maya villages it is a staple for gatherings and family meals.

Cacao and chocolate

The ancient Maya drank cacao with water, chile and spices long before chocolate bars existed. Belize’s cacao is grown largely by small farmers in Toledo, and several family operations near Punta Gorda and San Ignacio offer tours where you roast and grind beans by hand. The Chocolate Festival of Belize in Toledo celebrates this heritage. A bar of Belizean chocolate is one of the best food souvenirs to take home.

Where to experience it

  • Cacao farm tours in Toledo (Punta Gorda area) and near San Ignacio.
  • The San Ignacio Saturday market for tamales, tortillas and produce.
  • Village homestays and cooking experiences in Toledo.
