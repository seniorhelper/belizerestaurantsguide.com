Food culture

Creole food: the everyday backbone of Belize

When Belizeans say "Belizean food" without naming a culture, they usually mean Creole cooking.

Belizean Creole culture grew from the meeting of enslaved Africans and British settlers in the logging camps of the 18th and 19th centuries, with Indigenous and Caribbean influences layered on top. Belize City and the central coast are its heartland, and Kriol is the country’s common language. The cooking is thrifty, filling and built around coconut, rice, beans, root vegetables and slow-cooked meat.

Rice and beans vs beans and rice

The distinction matters. Rice and beans means red kidney beans and rice cooked together in coconut milk, often with a little salted pig tail for flavor, so every grain comes out fragrant. Beans and rice means white rice with stewed beans served separately. Ask for whichever you want; vegetarians should ask whether meat or lard went into the pot.

Stew chicken

Stew chicken is the companion to rice and beans and the closest thing to a national dish. Chicken is seasoned with red recado (an annatto paste), browned until the sugar in the pan darkens, then simmered into a rich, slightly sweet red-brown gravy. Lunch plates add potato salad or coleslaw and fried plantain.

Breakfast: fry jacks and johnny cakes

Fry jacks are triangles or pockets of dough that puff up in hot oil, eaten with refried beans, eggs, cheese, jam or honey. Johnny cakes are smaller and denser, baked with coconut milk and split for ham or cheese. Both turn up at every breakfast counter in the country.

Weekend comfort: boil up and cow foot soup

Boil up is the Kriol one-pot: fish or salted pig tail simmered with cassava, sweet potato, plantain, yam, boiled eggs and flour dumplings, then served with a tomato sauce poured over the top. Cow foot soup is a rich, gelatinous weekend special. You will find both at local diners and family kitchens rather than resort menus.

Sweets

Creole bakeries sell coconut tarts, bread pudding, powder buns and dense, rum-soaked rum cake, a favorite souvenir.

Where to try it

Any local diner in Belize City, San Pedro or San Ignacio will have rice and beans with stew chicken at lunch. For named places, see our sister site’s listings for San Pedro, Caye Caulker, Placencia, Hopkins and San Ignacio.

Dishes to know

Rice and beans

Red kidney beans and rice cooked together in coconut milk, the base of the national plate.

Typically vegetarian. Usually gluten-free.

Stew chicken

Chicken browned and simmered in a dark red sauce colored with recado.

Usually gluten-free.

Fry jacks

Puffed pillows of fried dough, filled or served with beans, eggs and cheese.

Typically vegetarian.

Johnny cakes

Small, dense baked rounds made with coconut milk, split and filled at breakfast.

Typically vegetarian.

Boil up

Fish or salted pig tail with cassava, plantain, sweet potato, eggs and dumplings under a tomato sauce.

Cow foot soup

A rich, gelatinous soup of cow foot, vegetables and dumplings, often a weekend special.

Meat pies

Small flaky pies of seasoned minced meat sold at shops and carts.

Gibnut

Paca, a large rodent; slow-cooked and nicknamed "the royal rat".

Usually gluten-free.

Rum cake

A moist cake soaked with Belizean rum, a popular gift and dessert.

Typically vegetarian.

Coconut tart

Sweet pastry filled with grated coconut, sold at bakeries.

Typically vegetarian.

Questions

What is boil up?

A Kriol one-pot of fish or salted pig tail, root vegetables, eggs and dumplings, served with tomato sauce. Look for it at local diners.

What is recado?

A seasoning paste. Red recado gets its color from annatto seeds and colors stew chicken and escabeche; black recado, made from charred chiles, is the base of relleno.
