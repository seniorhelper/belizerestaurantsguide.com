Belize seafood seasons: lobster and conch, month by month

Belize protects spiny lobster and queen conch with closed seasons. Pick a month to see what is legal to order.

The rules in plain English

Spiny lobster: open from July 1 to the end of February. Closed from March 1 to June 30. These dates come from Statutory Instrument 128 of 2021, which also aligns Belize with a regional rule across Central America. The 2026–27 season opened on July 1, 2026, and runs to February 28, 2027. Older guides (including earlier versions of this site) said June 15 to February 14; that is out of date.

Size rules apply: lobsters need a carapace of at least three inches and a tail of at least four ounces, and soft-shell or egg-bearing females are illegal to keep. Restaurants may not hold filleted or diced tails, which is why you see whole tails on menus.

Queen conch: opens on October 1 and is scheduled to close on June 30, but the Fisheries Department closes it earlier when the national production quota is reached. The 2025–26 season was cut short by more than two months for that reason. Conch is fully closed from July 1 to September 30.

What to order instead

Out of season, choose snapper, grouper, barracuda or shrimp, often fried whole or grilled with lime. If lobster or conch appears on a menu during the closed months, ask where it came from; frozen stock from before the closure may be legal for a short time, but it is fair to ask.

Lobsterfest

As the season opens in July, Lobsterfest celebrations take over San Pedro, Caye Caulker and Placencia with food stalls, music and lobster cooked every way. Check local news for this year’s dates. See our festival guide.
